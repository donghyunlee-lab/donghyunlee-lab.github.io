Our lab received the **Outstanding Abstract Award** at the **20th-anniversary 2026 Fall Meeting of the Korean Society for Zoonoses (KSZ)**, held on September 10–11 in Busan and co-hosted by the Animal and Plant Quarantine Agency.

The awarded study, "Forecasting country-level outbreaks of highly pathogenic avian influenza from international notification data: Supporting risk-based surveillance centered on subtype-linked transmission routes," was authored by **Hyeongseo Jeon** (first author, M.S. student) and **Prof. Donghyun Lee** (corresponding). Congratulations, Hyeongseo! 🎊

---

### What we presented

- An **ontology graph** linking virus subtypes and cross-country outbreak relations, used to forecast country-level HPAI outbreaks **two weeks ahead** from international notifications.
- The forecasts were driven not by outbreaks in neighboring countries but by **whether the same subtype is circulating internationally** — suggesting that risk-based surveillance should follow subtype groups rather than geographic adjacency.
- A premise we stated up front: notification data arrive with a lag and recent weeks are under-reported, so every early warning built on this data, ours included, stands on that condition. Probability calibration and external validation remain follow-up work.

Our thanks to the society's organizers and to the animal health authorities, physicians, veterinarians, and researchers who joined the discussion.
